Privacy policy

Last updated: 1 November 2026

Blandis is a free browser game. You can play without an account and without giving any personal data. There is no advertising, and analytics (Google Analytics) is only used if you accept it. This policy explains the personal data involved when you visit the game and, above all, when you choose to join the public global leaderboard.

1. Data controller

Controller: Joel

Contact for privacy matters: ice.dev1234@gmail.com

2. What data is processed, why, and on what legal basis

2.1 Global leaderboard (optional, 14 and over)

Nothing is sent to the leaderboard until, after a game, you type a nickname, tick the box confirming that you are 14 or older and accept the Terms and this policy, and press Send. From then on, each finished game is sent automatically with that nickname, until you clear your name in Settings or delete your global score.

Each time a game is sent, your browser transmits:

DataPurposeStoredPublic
Nickname you chooseShow your entry in the leaderboardYesYes
Game mode, score and game durationRank entries in that mode's leaderboard and check that the result is plausibleYes (best run per mode only)Yes
Player identifierKeep only your best run and let you delete your entryYesNo

The server also records the date of your best run (to order equal scores) and the date of your last submitted game (to apply the retention period in section 3). Neither is public.

The player identifier is a random code created in your browser the first time you send a score. It does not contain your name or information about your device, but it is still personal data, because it singles out your entry (pseudonymous data). We recommend that you do not use your real name as a nickname.

Legal basis: the processing is necessary to provide the leaderboard that you ask to join under the Terms (Article 6(1)(b) GDPR). Only the data needed for that purpose is processed.

2.2 Abuse prevention (IP address)

When a game is sent, the server stores your IP address (for IPv6, only its first half, which identifies your network) with the time of the request, to accept only one submission every 10 seconds from the same address. To limit how many new leaderboard entries can come from one address, it also keeps a code derived from your IP address (a keyed hash, from which the address can't be read back) with a count of new entries made from it in the last 24 hours. Neither is linked to your nickname, identifier or score.

When a game starts, the game asks the server for a signed code with the start time, and sends it back with the score. The server stores nothing when it hands out the code; once it has been used, only the code itself is kept, to refuse it a second time.

Legal basis: legitimate interest in protecting the leaderboard against spam and automated abuse (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR).

The server also checks every score automatically and rejects results that a real game cannot produce. This only decides whether a score enters the leaderboard; it has no legal or similarly significant effect on you.

2.3 Delivering the website

When you open the game or the leaderboard, your browser sends technical data (such as your IP address and browser type) to the providers that deliver them (section 4).

Legal basis: legitimate interest in delivering the game securely (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR).

2.4 Analytics (optional, only with your consent)

If you accept analytics in the banner or in Settings → Analytics, the game loads Google Analytics, which sets cookies with a random identifier (see the Cookie policy) and sends Google: the pages you open, when and for how long, how you got here (referring website), your browser, device type, screen size and language, and your approximate location (country and region), which Google works out from your IP address. Google states that Google Analytics does not log or store IP addresses of users in the European Union. Google signals and ad personalisation are turned off, and no nickname, score or player identifier is sent.

We only see aggregated statistics (for example, number of visits per country or device); we cannot tell who you are.

Legal basis: your consent (Article 6(1)(a) GDPR). You can withdraw it at any time in Settings → Analytics, which stops Google Analytics and deletes its cookies. Withdrawing does not affect what was processed before. If you don't accept, the game works exactly the same.

2.5 Data stored only on your device

Your progress, settings, nickname and player identifier are saved in your browser's local storage, as described in the Cookie policy. This data is not sent anywhere, except for the leaderboard data in section 2.1.

3. How long data is kept

  • Leaderboard entry (one per game mode): until you delete it, or automatically 12 months after the last game you sent in that mode, whichever comes first. The server checks this every hour.
  • IP address for abuse prevention: deleted after the 10-second window; an automatic clean-up runs every hour, so it is kept for about one hour at most.
  • Code derived from the IP address and its count of new entries: deleted 24 hours after the first new entry from that address (clean-up every hour).
  • Used game codes: deleted 12 hours after they were handed out (clean-up every hour).
  • Analytics: the cookies last 2 years unless you withdraw consent or delete them; Google Analytics keeps the detailed statistics for 2 months and only aggregated reports afterwards.
  • Data on your device: until you clear your browser's site data.

Request logging (Workers Logs) is turned off for the leaderboard server. The providers in section 4 may keep their own technical logs under their own policies.

5. Who can see your data

Anyone can see the public leaderboards (one per game mode): nickname, score and game duration of the top 50 entries of each. The player identifier, dates and IP address are never shown or shared. Analytics statistics are only seen by us. Data is not sold or used for advertising.

6. Your rights

Under the GDPR and the Spanish LOPDGDD you have the right to access, rectify and erase your data, to restrict or object to its processing, and to data portability.

  • Erase your leaderboard entries: in the game, go to Settings → Delete my global score. Your entries in every game mode are removed from the server immediately, and the nickname and identifier are removed from your device.
  • Change your nickname: change it in Settings; the leaderboard is updated the next time a game is sent. You can also delete your entry and join again.
  • Other requests: write to ice.dev1234@gmail.com. Include your player identifier, which you can find in your browser's local storage for this site under the key blandis-v2. We will answer within one month.

We do not know who players are. The player identifier is the only way to verify that an entry is yours, so requests about an entry can only be handled with it. Entries cannot be deleted on request using only a nickname or a score, because anyone could claim them. If you have lost your identifier (for example after clearing your browser data), your entry will be deleted automatically 12 months after the last game you sent (Article 11 GDPR).

If you think your data has not been handled correctly, you can complain to the Spanish Data Protection Agency (www.aepd.es) or to the data protection authority of your country.

7. Children

Anyone can play Blandis. The global leaderboard is only for players aged 14 or over, who must confirm their age before sending their first score. If we learn that an entry belongs to someone under 14, we will delete it.

Analytics is optional. If you are under 14, ask a parent or guardian before accepting it.
